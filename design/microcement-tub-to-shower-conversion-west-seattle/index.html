Who designed and built it

The owner set the brief for a durable rental bathroom. DuetRight built the shower and carried the material and wood details through the room.

Following the old shower door

The before photograph shows why the tub enclosure was awkward to live with. A tall tub wall and a sliding glass door occupied the same narrow opening. The metal track collected grime. Inside, a change from tile to tub added another joint to look after. The owner wanted to simplify what a tenant would use every day and what he would have to maintain between tenancies.

A fixed piece of glass could take the place of the slider. Microcement would let us finish the wall, pan and bathroom floor in the same warm tone. Taking those two decisions together removed the overhead door track and let the floor run visually into the shower.

Building the pan below the finish

The old tub and its surround came out to the studs. In their place we built a sloped mortar-bed pan with a PVC liner and waterproofed the shower. We held water in the pan for 24 hours before applying the finish. The photograph of the crew working on the pan records the part of this room that disappeared under the microcement.

We hand-applied two coats to the shower walls, then carried the finish across the pan and onto the main floor. Once it cured, we sealed it with a matte polyurethane topcoat. A fixed frameless panel went in after the plumbing and valve trim were fitted and tested.

Keeping the mirror and the counter

Across from the shower sat a vanity that could still serve the room. We surfaced the existing counter in a matching tone, changed the pulls and fitted a low-profile matte black faucet. A black light above the mirror repeats that finish.

The existing mirror stayed. We built a frame for it in oak. In the completed vanity photograph, the oak frame echoes the cabinets, and the pale counter carries the shower finish across the room. Reusing the mirror and counter also kept two serviceable pieces out of the demolition pile.

What a tenant sees at the door

The new sightline goes to a rain head and a single pane of clear glass. Below it, the sloped shower floor and the surrounding floor read as one surface. The finished photographs show that continuity in daylight and under the vanity light. The owner gained a bathroom with fewer edges and tracks to clean, along with the open shower he wanted to offer his tenants.
