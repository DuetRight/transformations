Making maintenance part of the layout

The old wood vanity stretched along the wall with the water heater hidden underneath. From the doorway it looked like useful storage. For the owner, it was a daily reminder that a leak could go unnoticed in a building with neighbors below. He wanted easy access and a simpler bathroom.

We relocated the heater and gave the sink its own slimmer cabinet. The finished photograph shows the result plainly. You can see the fixture, the heater and the space to reach it. Leaving that equipment accessible was a deliberate choice the owner could judge as soon as he entered the room.

Preserving the tub in a small footprint

The room still needed to work as both a bathtub and a shower. With the old surround out, the construction photos show the new tub sitting in exposed framing. That was the moment to locate a storage niche between the studs and waterproof it as part of the wall.

The homeowner helped choose the materials. The 12-by-24-inch porcelain selection gave the walls a broad, quiet surface. The niche provides a shelf within that surface for bottles. Epoxy grout, dark floor tile and black fixtures complete the material palette. The faucet and shower controls are easy to pick out against the light walls.

Moving storage to where it is used

A slimmer vanity worked because the room gained storage elsewhere. The tub has the recessed shelf, and a mirror cabinet keeps small items above the sink. A quieter exhaust fan with an integrated light addressed ventilation at the same time. Each piece does something the original large cabinet couldn't do on its own.

The owner followed the work through project updates and could see the tub, surround and fittings take shape. That mattered in a condo bathroom being taken apart and put back together in less than two weeks.

The photograph worth comparing

Look at the same wall before and after. In the first view the old vanity conceals nearly everything below the counter. In the second, the owner can walk straight to the heater and still has a cabinet at the sink. He told us the room felt bigger. The change he had asked for was access, and you can see where it came from.
